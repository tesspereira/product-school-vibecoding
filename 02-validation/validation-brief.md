# Validation Brief

> Module 2 · Validation. Frame the riskiest assumption, then build to test it. **This locks your scenario, no switching after M2.**

## Scenario

Tool Catalog at FakeCompany (own product)

## Riskiest assumption

_The one belief that, if false, sinks the idea._

The riskiest thing I'm assuming is that the flow is intuitive and the tool information is useful to the employee who is trying to decide what is the right tool for them.

## Real data (metrics): 
- 80% of Help Desk tickets are inquiries asking about what tools are approved or unapproved
- Low user satisfaction score related to company tools
- 50 complaints that tools are hard to find and take a long time to request

## User voice (quotes): 
- “I don’t know what is available for me to use.”
- “There are many options of tools and I don’t know how to compare and pick the right one.”
- “What am I even allowed to use for my client work?”
- “How can I see all the tools available? I don’t know what I need and I just want to browse to start.”
- “What’s not approved? I would rather know up front what’s banned before going down the path of requesting it and filling out forms just to be told “no”.” 

## Hypothesis

> We believe **providing certain information about tools up front in a simple manner** will cause **employees to understand what's available to them and more easily decide if it's appropriate for their needs** for **FakeCompany employees**. We'll know we're right when **the time it takes to find the right tool is less than 5 minutes on average**.

## Risk type

- [ ] Value (do they want it?)
- [x] Usability (can they use it?)
- [x] Feasibility (can we build it?)
- [ ] Viability (should we?)

## Kill switch

_The result that would make you stop or pivot._

If the prototype shows people seem stuck in an endless search loop, then I pivot.
